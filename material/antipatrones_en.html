Anti-pattern gallery

The exam lives on answers that sound right but are wrong. Here they are together: first the ones you can watch fail in the labs, then the typical misconceptions from the five guides.

Each case says whether it's an 🎓 Exam trap (Sounds right, but on the exam it is the wrong answer.) or a 🔧 Product limitation (It fails today because of how the product works. On the exam it is not the wrong answer: answer as the guide says.)

In the labs: watch them fail

Demo 1.1 · ❌ Stop when text appears 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: If Claude already wrote text, it has finished answering.

Why it fails: the loop stops as soon as the response contains text. Claude often writes “Let me check…” together with a tool_use, so some tools never run.

🎓 On the exam: “The agent stops too early” → check that the loop decides by stop_reason, not by the text.

Demo 1.1 · ❌ Out of room (max_tokens) 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: If the loop exited without an error, Claude finished.

Why it fails: With 40 tokens the response gets cut off: stop_reason = max_tokens. Claude did NOT finish, it ran out of room. For the exam the rule is the same even if you only see tool_use and end_turn: if it isn't end_turn, it didn't finish.

🎓 On the exam: Anything other than end_turn means “not done yet, check why”.

View recording
Demo 1.1 · ❌ Cap too low 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: An iteration cap is how you control when the loop ends.

Why it fails: With 2 iterations, the safety net cuts the loop before Claude finishes the refund.

🎓 On the exam: Favorite distractor: “raise the iteration cap”. The cap only guards against infinite loops.

View recording
Demo 1.2 · ❌ Narrow decomposition 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: If the report is missing topics, the subagents did a poor job.

Why it fails: The prompt pushes the coordinator to look only at marketing: nobody researches inventory, logistics or pricing.

🎓 On the exam: If the final report is missing a whole topic, the root cause is the coordinator's decomposition.

Demo 1.3 · ❌ Findings in prose (A) 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: A prose summary is enough to pass the findings to the synthesizer.

Why it fails: In a paragraph, sources and dates get lost: the synthesizer ends up writing [no source].

🎓 On the exam: To keep sources, the subagent returns structured metadata (claim + source + date).

Demo 1.3 · ❌ Subagents in sequence (B) 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Running the subagents one at a time makes no difference.

Why it fails: Here the coordinator requests the 3 delegations together, but your code runs them one after another: the total time is the sum of all of them. It's the same as when the coordinator launches one Task, waits, and then launches the next.

🎓 On the exam: Independent, slow subagents → several Task calls in a single response, in parallel.

Demo 1.3 · ❌ No Agent/Task in allowed_tools (C) 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Defining the subagent in agents is enough for the coordinator to use it.

Why it fails: The subagent is defined, but the coordinator isn't allowed to use the tool that launches it.

🎓 On the exam: Without Task (Agent) in allowedTools there are no subagents, even if they are defined.

Demo 1.4 · ❌ Rule only in the prompt 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: If the system prompt says “never refund more than $10,000 without approval”, Claude will comply.

Why it fails: The rule lives in the system prompt: under customer pressure, nothing guarantees it will be followed.

🎓 On the exam: Whatever must always hold (money, security) goes in code: a prerequisite gate or hook, not just the prompt.

Demo 1.4 · ❌ Prompt only, 5 attempts 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: If Claude followed the rule in my test, it will always follow it.

Why it fails: The same conversation 5 times: count how many times the rule was skipped. A single attempt proves nothing.

🎓 On the exam: Working almost always isn't enough for money or security: it goes in code.

Demo 1.4 · ❌ Free-text handoff 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: As long as it escalates to a human, it did its job.

Why it fails: It escalates with a loose note: whoever receives the case is missing data and can't see the chat.

🎓 On the exam: The human can't see the chat: the handoff carries customer_id, summary, root_cause, refund_amount and recommended_action.

Demo 1.5 · ❌ No PreToolUse 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Asking Claude in the prompt to respect the limit is enough.

Why it fails: Nothing stops the refund that exceeds the limit.

🎓 On the exam: Money, security or compliance → a PreToolUse hook that always blocks it, without depending on the model.

Demo 1.5 · ❌ No PostToolUse 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Claude will understand any format the carrier returns.

Why it fails: Claude gets dates and statuses in each carrier's raw formats.

🎓 On the exam: Mixed data formats → PostToolUse that normalizes them before Claude sees them.

Demo 1.5 · ❌ Block with PostToolUse 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Any hook works for blocking a tool.

Why it fails: The hook that was supposed to stop the refund runs AFTER: the money is already gone.

🎓 On the exam: To prevent an action, use PreToolUse (before it runs). PostToolUse only changes what Claude sees afterwards.

Demo 1.6 · ❌ Single pass, 14 files (B) 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: A single review of everything together is enough.

Why it fails: Reviewing 14 files at once: the cross-file bug is at the end and gets missed more often.

🎓 On the exam: Last parts poorly reviewed → multi-pass: one pass per part + one integration pass.

Demo 1.7 · ❌ Resume with a changed file (B) 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Resuming the session is always better: it already has all the context.

Why it fails: The old conversation carries data that has since changed in the file.

🎓 On the exam: If much of what it already analyzed changed → new session with a summary of what is current. If little changed → --resume and tell it which files changed.

Demo 2.1 · ❌ Minimal descriptions 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: The tool's name is enough for Claude to know when to use it.

Why it fails: With one line per tool, Claude confuses get_customer with lookup_order.

🎓 On the exam: Claude picks a tool by its description: if they get confused, the first fix is to improve the descriptions.

Demo 2.1 · ❌ Conflicting rule in the system prompt 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: A keyword rule in the system prompt helps pick the tool.

Why it fails: A keyword rule beats good descriptions.

🎓 On the exam: Keyword rules can contradict the descriptions and cause errors.

Demo 2.2 · ❌ “Operation failed” 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: A generic error message is enough; Claude will know what to do.

Why it fails: A generic error doesn't say whether to retry; and a system that's down returns an empty list, as if there were no orders.

🎓 On the exam: Errors with errorCategory and isRetryable; and a valid empty result (no orders) is not an error.

Demo 2.3 · ❌ One agent with 18 tools (A) 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: More tools = more capabilities = a better agent.

Why it fails: With 18 similar tools, it picks wrong more often and every request costs more tokens.

🎓 On the exam: Few tools per role (4-5). With 18, selection gets worse and the definitions travel with every request.

Demo 2.4 · ❌ Token written in .mcp.json (C) 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: If the .mcp.json works with the token inside, it's done.

Why it fails: The token ends up in the repo: anyone who clones it can see it.

🎓 On the exam: Secrets go in as ${VAR}: each person sets them in their own environment; the .mcp.json goes into git.

Demo 2.4 · ❌ Server only in ~/.claude.json (C) 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: If it works for me, it works for the team.

Why it fails: It works on your machine, but your teammate doesn't have the server.

🎓 On the exam: What the team shares goes in the project's .mcp.json; ~/.claude.json is personal.

Demo 2.5 · ❌ No Grep 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Glob is enough to find where a function is used.

Why it fails: Without searching content, it doesn't find every usage of processRefund.

🎓 On the exam: Who calls this function? → Grep (searches content). Files by name → Glob.

Demo 2.5 · ❌ No Glob 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Grep works just as well for listing files by name pattern.

Why it fails: Without searching by name, it struggles to list the *.test.ts files.

🎓 On the exam: Files by name pattern → Glob; content → Grep.

Demo 3.1 · ❌ New teammate 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: If an instruction works on your machine, it works for the whole team.

Why it fails: Without your CLAUDE.local.md, the teammate loses those instructions.

🎓 On the exam: CLAUDE.local.md and ~/.claude aren't shared: what's for the team goes in the project's CLAUDE.md.

Demo 3.1 · ❌ Without importing with @path 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Having docs/estilo.md in the repo is enough for Claude to follow it.

Why it fails: docs/estilo.md doesn't get loaded: the style rules are lost.

🎓 On the exam: To split the CLAUDE.md without duplicating, import with @path (there is no @import).

Demo 3.2 · ❌ Vague description (B) 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: It's enough for the skill to exist; Claude will use it when needed.

Why it fails: “Utilidades varias.” (miscellaneous utilities): Claude doesn't know when to use the skill.

🎓 On the exam: Claude decides to use a skill by its description: it must say what it does and when to use it.

Demo 3.3 · ❌ Rules without paths 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Having every convention always loaded costs nothing.

Why it fails: All the rules load at the start, whether they apply or not.

🎓 On the exam: Conventions for a file type spread across many folders → .claude/rules/ with paths.

Demo 3.4 · ❌ Direct with an ambiguous task 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: If the agent is good, it can decide on its own how to make the change.

Why it fails: It edits right away and picks a design on its own, without you reviewing it.

🎓 On the exam: When there are several valid approaches (design decisions) or the change is large, plan mode first.

Demo 3.4 · ❌ Plan mode for a clear change 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Planning never hurts: better use plan mode for a small change too.

Why it fails: Planning a change that has only one correct way just adds steps.

🎓 On the exam: Plan mode is for large, ambiguous or architectural changes. A clear, well-scoped change goes with direct execution.

Demo 3.5 · ❌ Prose only 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: A good prose description is enough for it to do it right.

Why it fails: A description in words gets interpreted differently: it fails on the edge cases.

🎓 On the exam: If it interprets your description differently → give concrete examples or failing tests.

Demo 3.6 · ❌ Text 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Text output can be processed in the pipeline just the same.

Why it fails: A machine can't read the output reliably.

🎓 On the exam: In CI: -p so it doesn't hang and --output-format json (with --json-schema) so a machine can read it.

Demo 4.1 · ❌ “Be conservative” 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Asking it to “be conservative” reduces false positives.

Why it fails: Without criteria, it flags too much or too little.

🎓 On the exam: Explicit criteria: what TO report and what NOT to, with examples.

Demo 4.2 · ❌ Without examples 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Instructions are enough for the ambiguous cases.

Why it fails: Instructions only: the ambiguous cases come out inconsistent.

🎓 On the exam: Ambiguous cases or inconsistent format → 2 to 4 few-shot examples with their reasoning.

Demo 4.3 · ❌ JSON requested in the prompt 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Asking “answer in JSON” in the prompt is enough.

Why it fails: “Answer in JSON”: it can come out broken or with invented data.

🎓 On the exam: Broken JSON → tool_use with a schema; nullable fields so it doesn't invent data.

Demo 4.4 · ❌ Generic error 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Telling it “there are errors, try again” is enough.

Why it fails: “There are errors, try again”: it repeats the same ones.

🎓 On the exam: Retry with the specific error; if the data isn't in the document, retrying doesn't help.

Demo 4.4 · ❌ No retry 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: What it returns first is usually right.

Why it fails: It keeps whatever comes back first, errors included.

🎓 On the exam: Validate in code and retry with the specific error.

Demo 4.5 · ❌ Match by order 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: The results arrive in the same order I sent them.

Why it fails: It assumes result 1 belongs to review 1: if they arrive in another order, they get mixed up.

🎓 On the exam: Order isn't guaranteed: always match by custom_id (that way you also resend only the ones that failed).

Demo 4.5 · ❌ Batch for a pre-merge review 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Batch is 50% cheaper: it's worth it for everything.

Why it fails: Someone is waiting on the review and a batch can take up to 24 h. The batch isn't sent.

🎓 On the exam: What blocks someone (pre-merge review) goes synchronous; Batch only for what can wait.

Demo 4.6 · ❌ Same session 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Asking Claude to review its own work in the same session is enough.

Why it fails: Reviewing itself in the same session lets errors slip through.

🎓 On the exam: Review with an independent instance: a new call that didn't see how it was written.

Demo 5.1 · ❌ Progressive summary (A) 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: A summary of the conversation keeps what matters.

Why it fails: The summary keeps erasing exact data: “your recent refund”.

🎓 On the exam: Key case data (amounts, dates, IDs) in a case facts block, outside the summary.

Demo 5.1 · ❌ Key fact in the middle (C) 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: If the fact is in the context, the model will find it.

Why it fails: The code is buried among 600 notes with similar codes (and other notes about the same order).

🎓 On the exam: Lost in the middle: what matters goes at the start or the end, with headers.

Demo 5.2 · ❌ Vague instruction 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Escalating when the customer is upset is good service.

Why it fails: It escalates because of anger and doesn't ask for data when the case is ambiguous.

🎓 On the exam: Escalate: they ask for a person, the policy doesn't cover the case, or no progress. Not because of anger; if it's ambiguous, ask for the missing data.

Demo 5.3 · ❌ Silent failure 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: If a subagent finds nothing, returning “no data” is correct.

Why it fails: “No relevant data” as if everything were fine.

🎓 On the exam: Subagent errors: structured (type, what it tried, partials, alternatives), never silent.

Demo 5.3 · ❌ Generic “Error” 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: Reporting “Error” is enough.

Why it fails: The coordinator knows something failed, but not what or what to do.

🎓 On the exam: The error must say what failed and what alternatives exist.

Demo 5.3 · ❌ Abort the whole pipeline 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: If a subagent fails, the safest thing is to stop everything.

Why it fails: A single failure stops everything: no report, not even with what did work.

🎓 On the exam: Keep going with what you have: the coordinator uses the partial results and notes the gap in the report.

Demo 5.4 · ❌ No notes 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: The new session will remember what was already explored.

Why it fails: The new session starts from zero and explores everything again.

🎓 On the exam: Long exploration → a scratchpad (NOTAS.md) that survives between sessions.

Demo 5.5 · ❌ Decide with the overall accuracy 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: With 97% overall accuracy you can already remove human review.

Why it fails: The overall number looks high and review is removed for everything: the errors in the notes slip through.

🎓 On the exam: Measure by document type (and by field) with stratified sampling: the overall number hides the category that fails.

Demo 5.5 · ❌ Trust uncalibrated confidence 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: If the model says 0.9 confidence, it's right 9 times out of 10.

Why it fails: Everything the model says it knows gets accepted: its confidence doesn't always tell the truth.

🎓 On the exam: Declared confidence isn't calibrated: calibrate it with labeled data before using it as a threshold.

Demo 5.6 · ❌ Free summary 🎓 Exam trap

Looks right: A good prose summary is enough.

Why it fails: Where each fact came from gets lost and one source is chosen without saying so.

🎓 On the exam: Each claim with source and date; if the sources disagree, note both.

🔧 Product limitation: they fail today, but they aren't wrong on the exam

It fails today because of how the product works. On the exam it is not the wrong answer: answer as the guide says.

Demo 2.3 · ❌ tool_choice any with Sonnet (B) 🔧 Product limitation

Looks right: tool_choice any / tool works the same on every model.

Why it fails: Sonnet 5.5 rejects any / tool with a 400 error.

🎓 On the exam: any forces it to use some tool and tool forces a specific one. Today Sonnet and Opus 5.5 reject them: that's the product, not the exam.

Typical misconceptions from the guides

1.1 · Agentic loop and stop_reason

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“If the response has text, it's done”Claude often writes “Looking up your order…” and asks for the tool in the same response. It's the slide “Bug: the loop ends too early”
“I look for the word done in the text”Language is ambiguous; the official signal is stop_reason
“An iteration cap controls the loop”It's only a safety net against infinite loops; never the normal exit condition
“The API remembers the conversation”It's stateless: if you don't send the history, Claude doesn't know what happened before
“Claude runs the tool”Claude asks for it; your code (or the Agent SDK) runs it

1.2 · Coordinator and subagents (hub-and-spoke)

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“The subagent sees what was already discussed”It only sees its prompt; if something isn't there, it doesn't exist for it
“A topic is missing → the subagent searched poorly”First check what the coordinator assigned it: the root cause is usually the decomposition
“Running everything through every subagent is safer”It's waste, even if you cache results; the coordinator picks only the ones it needs
“Subagents can coordinate among themselves”No: everything goes through the coordinator

1.3 · Invoking subagents and passing them context

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“The subagent can look up what another one found”There's no shared memory: if it isn't pasted into its prompt, it doesn't have it
“The summary is enough”Without the metadata (URL, page, date) nobody can cite later
“Giving it detailed steps makes it more reliable”It makes it rigid: with a goal it adapts when something fails
“To run them in parallel you need to configure something special”It's enough for the coordinator to emit several Task calls in the same response

1.4 · Enforcement and handoff

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“With a very well-written prompt the gate isn't needed”The prompt is probabilistic; for money or security you need a guarantee
“Adding few-shot verification examples solves it”It improves the rate, but it still isn't a guarantee
“When escalating, the human can read the conversation”In the exam scenario they can't: the summary must be enough
“A message with 3 topics is handled topic by topic”It's decomposed, researched in parallel and answered in a single response

1.5 · Hooks: PreToolUse and PostToolUse

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“I can block a refund with PostToolUse”By the time it runs, the action already happened. Blocking is PreToolUse's job
“I can normalize the result with PreToolUse”PreToolUse doesn't see the result: it doesn't exist yet
“The hook asks Claude not to do it”It doesn't ask anything: the SDK doesn't run the tool, no matter what Claude wants
“Hooks and the 1.4 gate are different things”It's the same idea (deterministic code); the hook is how it's done when the SDK runs the loop

1.6 · Task decomposition

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“Dynamic decomposition is always better because it's smarter”For a task with known steps, it's less predictable without gaining anything
“If the review misses bugs, I use a bigger model”It's a structural problem: you have to split it into passes
“Splitting into batches solves attention dilution”Only if you add the integration pass; without it, the bugs between batches get lost
“The integration pass reviews everything again”It reviews only relationships between files, not the local bugs

1.7 · Sessions: resume, fork and stale context

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“If I resume and tell it to reread the file, it's fixed”The old fact is still in the history next to the new one
“fork_session is for starting clean”It copies the whole history, including what's stale
“Forking modifies the original session”No: the original stays intact; the branch has another id
“It's always better to start from scratch”If almost everything is still valid, --resume + saying what changed is cheaper and keeps the work

2.1 · Tool design: descriptions and boundaries

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“Claude knows what the tool does because it sees the code”It only sees name, description and input_schema
“The first thing is to add few-shot examples”They treat the symptom and spend tokens; descriptions first
“With good descriptions the system prompt no longer matters”A keyword sentence can beat them
“Renaming requires rewriting the function”Only the interface changes (name + description)

2.2 · Structured errors

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“If it fails, I return an empty list and that's it”Claude takes it as a success and says something false
“An empty result is an error”If the query worked, it's a success with 0 results
“validation is retryable”The malformed ID fails the same way every time: the input has to be fixed
“isRetryable: false = give up”It means don't repeat that call; it can fix it, take another path or escalate
“The subagent must send every error up to the coordinator”First it retries what's transient; it sends up only what it couldn't resolve, with what it tried

2.3 · Tool distribution and tool_choice

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“More tools = a more capable agent”More tools = worse selection and more tokens in every request
“If there's misrouting, you always have to reduce tools”With few tools that look alike, improving descriptions is enough
“Giving synthesis full search access solves the latency”It breaks the separation of roles; the narrow verify_fact tool does solve it
“any forces a specific tool”any forces some tool; the specific one is forced

2.4 · Integrating MCP servers

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“If the repo is private, I can paste the token”It stays in the history of every clone and fork; use ${VAR}
“The team's config goes in ~/.claude.json”That one is personal; the team's goes in .mcp.json in git
“Resources and tools are the same thing”Resources show what's there; tools act
“If it ignores my MCP tool, I remove Grep”Improve your tool's description; removing built-ins breaks other things
“It's always better to build my own server”For standard integrations, a community one first

2.5 · Claude Code built-in tools

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“Glob finds who calls a function”Glob only sees file names; for content it's Grep
“Read + Write is the normal way to edit”It's the fallback when Edit can't find a unique anchor
“A Grep for the name finds every usage”Aliases and re-exports hide places that call it
“Reading the whole repo at the start gives better context”It fills the window with irrelevant files

3.1 · CLAUDE.md hierarchy

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“The most specific CLAUDE.md wins”There's no precedence: they're concatenated
“If I put it in CLAUDE.md, it always holds”It's guidance; for a guarantee, settings.json or a hook
“Splitting with @ saves tokens”Everything loads the same; it only improves maintenance
“It's written @import”It's @ + path
“/memory loads the files”It only shows them; what loads depends on where the file is

3.2 · Slash commands and skills

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“.claude/skills/review.md creates /review”In skills you need the review/ folder with SKILL.md inside (.claude/skills/review/SKILL.md)
“A skill is always loaded”Only its description; the body enters when invoked
“The team's standards go in a skill”They go in CLAUDE.md, which is always present
“Without a description it still gets invoked on its own”Without a description it only runs if you type it (/review); Claude doesn't know when to use it

3.3 · Path rules (.claude/rules with paths)

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“Test conventions go in the root CLAUDE.md”They would always load, even if you don't touch tests
“A CLAUDE.md in every test folder”They're copies that drift out of sync
“A rule without paths loads only when needed”Without paths it always loads
“Rules and skills are the same thing”The rule is guidance in context; the skill is a flow that gets invoked

3.4 · Plan mode vs. direct execution

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“Hard task = plan mode”What decides is whether design decisions remain
“I'll start direct and switch to plan if it gets complicated”If you already know it's complex, plan from the start
“Writing ‘in plan mode’ in the prompt turns it on”You have to switch modes (flag, Shift+Tab or /plan)
“Plan mode is slower, better always go direct”On open-ended tasks it avoids redoing work

3.5 · Iterative refinement

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“If it doesn't understand, I rewrite the instruction more precisely”Prose always gets interpreted; use examples
“The interview works for any task”It's for when you don't know what matters; if you know the result, examples
“It's always better to send one fix at a time”If they interact, they go together
“Many examples is better”2–3 pairs are enough; then check with a new case

3.6 · CI/CD with claude -p

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“--batch or CLAUDE_HEADLESS exists”They don't exist; it's -p
“The same session can review what it generated”It carries over its reasoning and defends its decisions
“Batch API for the pre-merge check, it's cheaper”It can take 24 h; if someone is waiting, real-time
“To avoid repeating findings, I review only the last commit”It hides what's pending; pass the previous findings as context
“--system-prompt for a code reviewer”It replaces everything; to add rules it's --append-system-prompt

4.1 · Explicit criteria in the system prompt

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“Be conservative” reduces false positivesDoesn't say what to report or what to ignore
“A confidence threshold filters out the bad ones”Uncalibrated confidence doesn't predict correctness
“Defining severity in clear prose is enough”It gets interpreted differently; use code examples
“A category with false positives only affects that category”The devs stop trusting the whole output

4.2 · Few-shot prompting

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“If it comes out inconsistent, I add more instructions”Few-shot usually works better
“The examples should be the easy cases”They should cover the cases that fail
“Few-shot fixes broken JSON”That's tool_use with a schema
“The more examples, the better”2–4 on the exam; more spends tokens

4.3 · Structured output with tool use

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“tool_use prevents all extraction errors”Only syntax ones; errors of meaning need validation
“Marking everything as required gives more complete data”It forces it to invent what isn't there
“With clear instructions, JSON from the prompt is reliable”It's still free text
“auto guarantees a tool call”It can answer with text; any or forced do guarantee it

4.4 · Validation, retry and feedback

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“If a piece of data is missing, I retry until it shows up”If it isn't in the document, no retry creates it
“‘Try again’ is enough”Without the concrete error it repeats the same error
“If there's a schema, no validation is needed”The schema doesn't validate business rules
“If two values conflict, I pick the more likely one”Extract both, flag the conflict and send to review

4.5 · Message Batches API

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“It's cheaper, so use it for everything”If someone is waiting, synchronous
“It usually takes minutes, I can count on that”Design for 24 h; there's no SLA
“The results arrive in order”They're matched by custom_id
“If some fail, I resend the whole batch”Resend only the errored or expired ones
“The batch can call my tools midway”No multi-turn tool calling; that step goes synchronous

4.6 · Independent-instance review and multi-pass

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“Asking it in the same session to review carefully is enough”It keeps its reasoning and confirms its decisions
“Extended thinking in the same session fixes it”The original reasoning is still in context
“With a bigger window I review the 14 files together”The problem is attention, not size
“The confidence the model reports is reliable”It has to be calibrated with labeled sets

5.1 · Context window: case facts, trimming and lost in the middle

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“Summarizing is safe for case data”It destroys amounts, dates and IDs
“I keep the full tool result just in case”It's paid for in every turn and dilutes attention
“‘Read everything carefully’ fixes lost in the middle”You have to move what's key to the top and structure it
“Prompt caching reduces what the model reads”It only reduces the cost

5.2 · Escalation and ambiguity

What people thinkWhy it's wrong
“If the customer is very upset, I escalate”Anger doesn't indicate complexity
“If they ask for a human, I try to resolve it first”Escalate immediately
“The model can self-assess its confidence to escalate”It's poorly calibrated
“Everything outside the policy gets escalated”If the policy forbids it, the agent rejects it; if it doesn't cover it, it gets escalated
“With several Juan Pérez I pick the most recent”Ask for more identifiers
